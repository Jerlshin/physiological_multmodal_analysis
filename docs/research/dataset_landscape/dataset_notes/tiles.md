# TILES family and 2025 sleep benchmark

**Disposition: important longitudinal alternative; secondary domain resource for this selection.** Sources: [official TILES project](https://tiles-data.isi.edu/homepage); [TILES-2019 descriptor, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01636-4); [TILES-2018 Sleep Benchmark preprint, July 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03520).

The 2025 sleep benchmark describes 139 hospital employees followed over ten weeks, over 6,000 sleep recordings and survey outcomes. TILES-2019 instead follows 57 ICU medical residents for three weeks with Fitbit, audio features, proximity/context, surveys and job evaluations. These are **distinct cohorts/releases**; do not merge their counts or invent simultaneous EEG/EDA coverage. Repeated IDs and ecological behavior are major strengths. Exact release-specific demographics, native versus vendor-derived rates, raw waveform coverage, synchronization precision, missingness, license, bytes and download approval were not verified here. Official documentation provides research access and family-specific descriptions; no uniform BIDS claim is made.

Supports within-person longitudinal behavior/well-being associations and temporal generalization more directly than single-session laboratory datasets. Cannot infer clinical PSG sleep truth from consumer sleep estimates, causal burnout mechanisms from observational surveys, or fine brain–body timing without corresponding raw sensors. Shift schedule, role, device algorithms, wear adherence and missing surveys confound associations.

The 2025 benchmark already evaluates sleep-stage classification, subjective sleep quality and demographics. Family literature is established; the new benchmark's incremental publication count was not established. Opportunities may concern measurement bias, adherence and longitudinal forecasting, but ordinary sleep prediction is occupied. DREAMT is preferable when an independent clinical sleep reference is essential; TILES can be preferable when naturalistic duration and occupational context are essential. Its nonselection does not imply that small laboratory datasets are always scientifically superior.
